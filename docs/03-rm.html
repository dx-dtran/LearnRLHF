Reward model

Turning "I liked this one better" into a differentiable number.

1Why pairs, not scores

You could ask labelers to rate responses 1–10 and regress on the rating. In practice this fails: people use scales differently, drift over a session, and disagree with themselves. What people are reliable at is comparison — "this one is better." So the dataset is pairs, and the model's job is only to make the chosen response score higher than the rejected one. The absolute numbers mean nothing; only gaps do. Keep that in mind for §4 — it explains the histograms.

2Architecture: one scalar, read at the right spot

The reward model is your SFT transformer with the vocabulary head swapped for Linear(768, 1). It emits a scalar at every position, but only one matters: the last real token, where the model has read the entire prompt and response.

Gotcha — problem 3.1 Reading the reward at a pad position is the RM's version of the loss-mask bug: the model trains, accuracy climbs a little (pads still attend to real tokens), and everything downstream — every PPO reward — is quietly computed from a garbage position.

3Bradley–Terry: a sigmoid on the gap

Score both responses, take the gap Δ = rc − rr, and interpret σ(Δ) as "probability the human prefers the chosen one." The loss is the negative log of that probability.

That equal-and-opposite structure is worth staring at: ∂L/∂rc = σ(Δ) − 1 and ∂L/∂rr = 1 − σ(Δ). The pair only ever moves the gap; the mean of the two rewards gets no gradient at all. This is why RM scores have no absolute meaning, and it is the symmetry your grad check in 3.3 should confirm.

Numerical stability Never compute -log(sigmoid(d)) literally — for very negative Δ, the sigmoid underflows to 0 and the log explodes. The same function is softplus(-d), which is stable everywhere. Same math, different overflow behavior; your fp64 grad check won't care, but bf16 training will.

4Reading the result

Two checks tell you the RM is real (problems 3.4–3.5):

  • Pairwise accuracy ≥ 65% on held-out pairs. That sounds low; it is roughly where InstructGPT's own RMs sat. Human agreement on HH is itself only ~70-something percent — you cannot beat the noise ceiling of your labels.
  • Overlapping histograms with a positive mean gap. If the two distributions separate cleanly, be suspicious: the RM may have found a shortcut (length, formatting) rather than quality. Shortcuts here become exploits in Module 5.

One more design choice to internalize: the RM is initialized from sft.pt, not from base GPT-2. It needs to already speak the chat format and judge responses in the same distribution the policy will actually produce — and a judge that shares the policy's representation of "good text" is a fairer target for optimization.
